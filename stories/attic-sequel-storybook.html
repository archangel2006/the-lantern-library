Wilbur and Theo Explore the Attic

A sequel from Number 13, Hollow Lane

Scroll to turn the page.

It was a rainy Saturday, and Theo had a question. “Wilbur, can we go up and see the attic properly? In the daytime?”

Wilbur had lived there for two hundred years, and nobody had ever asked for a tour. He floated up to his full height, and made a very small flag out of a sock.

There was so much to see. Trunks and hat stands, a box of buttons older than Theo’s grandmother, and chairs under white sheets that looked suspiciously like ghosts.

“No relation,” said Wilbur. “Very rude.”

Suddenly, from the corner, came a slow, long creak. Creeeak. Creeeak. Wilbur dived behind Theo. Theo dived behind Wilbur. It would have been funny if anyone had been watching.

The creaking came from the old rocking horse, and on its back, rocking happily, sat a small mouse called Pepper.

“Is there anything else up here that needs introducing?” asked Theo. Wilbur thought about it. “Well,” he said, “there’s that.”

Under the biggest dust sheet in the attic stood a long brass telescope. “It points at the sky,” said Wilbur. “But I’ve never looked. The sky is so very dark.”

So they waited for dusk. Theo brought a blanket, a mug of cocoa, and his best flashlight. Wilbur brought his knees, which knocked.

Together they opened the little round window, and Theo pointed the telescope at the first small star.

“Go on,” said Theo. Wilbur took a deep breath, which is a thing ghosts can pretend to do, and put his eye to the glass.

He saw thousands of lights. Thousands and thousands of them. “But it isn’t empty at all,” he whispered. “The dark is full of night-lights.”

“Look,” said Theo, pointing at five bright stars in a zigzag. “That one is shaped like a W.”

“W,” said Wilbur slowly. “As in…?” “As in Wilbur.” And the ghost glowed so brightly that the whole attic turned silver, and the mouse had to squint.

Now, on every clear night, the three of them sit in the round window: a boy, a ghost, and a mouse with a thimble of cocoa.

Wilbur still glows, but he is not afraid of the dark anymore. The attic, which used to be the loneliest room in the house, has become the best seat in it.
