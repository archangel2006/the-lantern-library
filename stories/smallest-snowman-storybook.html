The Smallest Snowman

A winter story about where the snow goes

Scroll to turn the page.

One winter morning, a girl named Ines made the smallest snowman in the world. He was no taller than a teacup, with two coal eyes, a carrot nose, and a scarf made from a strip of red ribbon.

She set him carefully on the windowsill and said, “You look after the garden.”

The snowman opened his eyes. It was very bright and very cold, which suited him perfectly. A sparrow landed beside him.

“Hello,” said the snowman. “I’m new. What happens next?” “Next,” said the sparrow, “is spring.”

“What’s spring?” asked the snowman. “Oh, it’s the best,” said the sparrow. “Flowers, and green, and warm days that go on and on.”

The snowman imagined it all, and wanted very much to see it. He did not know that he was made of exactly the stuff that spring takes away.

The days grew longer. The sun grew warmer. Each morning the snowman felt a little smaller, and each noon there was another drip from the windowsill.

“Is this normal?” he asked. The sparrow looked at the sky, and was very quiet.

“I think I’m going,” said the snowman, who was now the size of a walnut. “And I haven’t seen spring.”

The sparrow fluffed up beside him. “You’re not going,” said the sparrow. “Snow never goes. It only travels.”

So the snowman, who was now one bright drop, rolled off the sill, slipped down the gutter, and set off.

He went down a stream and along a river, past ducks and a mill and one very surprised frog, and out into the wide blue sea.

In the sea, he was warm for the first time. The sun lifted him up, as a mist, higher than the rooftops, and he became a cloud with two coal-dark eyes.

Below him, the garden was full of crocuses and green and yellow, and he saw every bit of it. It was spring after all.

When winter came again, a single snowflake landed on Ines’s windowsill, and she looked at it closely. In its middle was one tiny thread of red.

She smiled, scooped up some snow, and made the smallest snowman in the world. “Welcome back,” she said.
