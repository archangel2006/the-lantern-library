Gus and the Birthday Cake Disaster

A very hot sequel from Crumb Hollow

Scroll to turn the page.

It was Marigold’s birthday, and Gus wanted to give her something special. “I shall bake her a cake,” he said, “all by myself.”

He had never baked anything before, except, by accident, a barn.

He measured the flour very carefully. Then: hic! The flour went up in a great white whoomph, and the kitchen looked as if it were snowing.

“That was attempt one,” said Gus, from underneath a bowl.

Attempt two went better, until a hiccup came halfway through.

The cake went into the oven as a cake. It came out as a very small, very black hat.

Mrs. Crumb, the baker, had an idea. “Gus, your hiccups come every seven seconds. If we count, we can aim them.”

So she held a stopwatch, and he faced the oven, and together they counted: “One, two, three, four, five, six…”

Hic! The oven glowed. Hic! The oven glowed again. And when the door opened, there was a perfect, golden cake with three round tiers.

“Gus,” said Mrs. Crumb, “you are a wonder.”

The icing, however, melted if Gus stood within three metres. So he decorated from across the village green, using the longest spoon in Crumb Hollow.

It took all afternoon. The cake got a hundred strawberries and not a single scorch mark.

At four o’clock, Marigold walked into the bakery, and the whole village jumped out from behind the barrels. “SURPRISE!”

She gazed at the cake. It was tall and golden and covered in strawberries. There was just one thing missing. “Does anyone have a match?” asked the mayor.

Hic! Nine little flames, exactly nine, popped onto nine little candles, and not one crumb was singed.

“Best hiccup ever,” said Marigold. She made a wish, blew them out, and the whole village agreed it was the finest birthday cake in Crumb Hollow. Even the barn was impressed.
