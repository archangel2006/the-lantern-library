The Night the Tide Got Lost

A bedtime story from Saltmarsh Light

Scroll to turn the page.

At the end of a causeway that the sea closed twice a day, there stood a lighthouse. In the top of it lived a girl named Mina and her grandfather, Osric.

The tide came in every evening like a big, shy animal, and went out every morning the same way. Mina had known it all her life and had never once seen it up close.

page 1

“Ships have maps,” said Osric, polishing the great lamp. “The lamp isn’t for them. It’s for the tide. The tide has no map at all, and it is terribly afraid of fog.”

Every dusk he lit the lamp, and every dusk the water found its way home along the beam.

page 2

Then, one October night, the fog came in thick as wool. It swallowed the beam whole. The tide went out, and it did not come back.

By midnight the sands lay bare and silent. The seals stopped singing. Even the gulls held their breath.

page 3

Osric was too slow for the 112 steps and the wet sand beyond them, and the lamp was no use in fog. So Mina put on her boots, took the small brass lantern from the kitchen window, and went out alone.

On the sand she found prints, wide as dinner plates and slowly filling with water. She followed them.

page 4

Far out, in a rock pool no bigger than a bathtub, something was curled up small. It was the tide. It was the size of a sheepdog, the colour of sea glass, and it was shivering.

“I can’t see the light,” it whispered, in a voice like water finding a drain. “I have looked and looked.”

page 5

Mina could not carry it. But she remembered the counting song Osric sang while he worked: one for the shore, two for the shell, three for the boat that is coming home well. The tide had always counted along without her knowing.

She held the lantern low, walked backwards, and sang. Step by step, the tide followed the little light.

page 6

At the edge of the sand the fog began to thin, because the tide was pushing it. Behind her the water rose, and rose, and came rushing up the flats all the way to the lighthouse door.

Up in the tower, Osric had been wiping the glass with his sleeve all night. At last the beam broke through, and the seals began to sing.

page 7

Now, when the tide goes out at night, Mina puts a small brass lantern on the sill, just in case.

If you ever stay at Saltmarsh Light, there will be one in your window too. Leave it burning.
