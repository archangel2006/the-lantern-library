The Fox Who Kept Echoes

A winter story from Hollow Valley

Scroll to turn the page.

High above Hollow Valley, in a cave with a very good view, lived a fox named Juniper who collected echoes.

She caught them in glass jars and stopped them with corks, and she kept them in a row along her ledge, where they glowed like small lamps.

She had the echo of the church bell, the long, round note that rolled down from the ridge. She had the echo of the waterfall, which sounded like someone laughing in a bucket. And she had the echo of wind in the pines, her favourite, though she could never say why.

The valley folk thought it a peculiar hobby. Juniper thought it was only good manners to keep what the valley forgot.

Then came the great snow. It fell for three days and three nights, and snow, as everyone knows, swallows sound.

The bell went mute. The waterfall froze. The pines stood still, with nothing at all to say.

Down in the white hush sat a young owl named Pell, who had gone out to practise flying and landed somewhere he did not know.

“Mama!” he hooted. But the snow ate the sound before it left his beak. Far away, his mother called back, and the snow ate that too.

Juniper, up in her cave, heard nothing, and heard it loudly. She looked at her jars. Then she carried them down to the ridge, pulled out the first cork, and the bell rolled across the whole valley, as big as it had ever been.

Pell’s mother heard it. She turned her head and flew toward the bell. Then Juniper opened the waterfall, and the owl turned again. Then she opened the wind in the pines, and the owl followed it down.

Pell heard them too, and stumbled toward every sound he knew. Bell, then water, then wind: the valley had a map again, made of sounds.

They met under the pines at dawn. The mother owl folded Pell inside her wings so completely that only his beak showed, and he said something small and muffled, which sounded very much like “sorry.”

Juniper watched from the ridge with her tail wrapped round her feet, and pretended she was not crying.

That night her ledge was empty, but the valley was not. The bell rang over the snow, the waterfall sang under the ice, and the pines spoke at last.

And when the owls said good night, Juniper was ready with a fresh jar, because it was the finest echo she had ever heard.
