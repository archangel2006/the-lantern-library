The Cheese Wheel Express

A mountain train adventure

Scroll to turn the page.

It was the biggest job Pip had ever been given. The Great Summit Cheese, round as a wagon wheel and twice as heavy, had to reach the mountaintop festival before sunrise.

“We’ll need a few helpers,” said Pip. So Barnaby the bear, Mrs. Hazel the goat, and Professor Hoot the owl all climbed aboard.

Up they went in the dark, past the sleeping pines, with the lamps in the windows glowing like a string of small suns.

“Easy does it,” whispered Pip. The cheese wheel smelled so strongly of mountain that even the stars seemed to lean in.

Halfway up, the fog came down so thick that Pip could not see the front of his own train. “I can’t see the track!” he cried.

“I can,” said Professor Hoot. His round eyes glowed like two small headlamps, and he called every bend in the dark. “Left! Right! A little left!” And the little train crept on.

Then came a great rumble. A rockslide had dropped a boulder the size of a haystack right across the line.

“Leave it to me,” said Barnaby, who was wide awake for once. He planted his big paws, took a breath, and pushed. The boulder groaned. Then it rolled off the edge, and did not come back.

Next, the old wooden bridge. One plank was missing, and below it, the chasm was so deep that the clouds were underneath.

“Gaps,” said Mrs. Hazel, “are what goats are for.” She trotted onto the bridge, leapt across in one graceful jump, and shoved the last plank into place with her horns.

The little train inched across, one wheel at a time. The bridge creaked. The cheese wheel wobbled. Nobody breathed.

“Easy,” murmured Pip. “Easy, easy, easy.” And then the front wheels touched solid ground, and the whole train let out a very long whistle of relief.

Just before the summit, a final bump jolted the flatcar. The Great Summit Cheese rolled off the back, and went bouncing down the slope.

“Stop that cheese!” cried Pip. But Barnaby was already there. He sat down in the middle of the track, opened his arms, and the great wheel rolled gently into his tummy and stopped.

The sun came up over the summit just as the little train puffed in, and the whole festival cheered.

The Great Summit Cheese was cut into triangles, of course, because Pip still believed in triangles. Everyone had a slice, and Barnaby had two. It had been a long night, and he had earned them both.
