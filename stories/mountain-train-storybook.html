The Great Sandwich Mystery

A story from the 4:15 mountain train

Scroll to turn the page.

Every afternoon at 4:15, the little red train climbed from Pinecone Station to the top of the world.

Its conductor was Pip, a marmot with a very shiny whistle, who always packed a hamper of sandwiches for the passengers. Six of them, cut into triangles, because Pip believed in triangles.

Today’s passengers were Mrs. Hazel the goat, Professor Hoot the owl, and a large, sleepy bear named Barnaby, who was going to visit his cousin.

“Sandwiches at the first tunnel!” called Pip, and everyone sat up very straight.

The train rolled into the first tunnel, and the whole world went black.

It lasted only a minute, with a clatter and a hum and the smell of cold stone. Then they were out in the light again, blinking.

“Sandwiches!” said Pip, lifting the lid of the hamper. And then he stopped.

The hamper was empty. There was nothing in it but crumbs, and a small smear of mustard. All six triangles had vanished in the dark.

“This,” said Pip, “is a case for a detective.” So at the second tunnel he hid under the table with a flashlight, and waited.

In the dark came a soft sound: munch, munch, munch. Pip clicked on the light. There stood Barnaby, fast asleep with his eyes shut, chewing a triangle.

The train burst out into the sunshine, and Barnaby woke up with mustard on his chin. “I had the strangest dream,” he said. “It was all about sandwiches.”

Everyone laughed so hard that the windows rattled. Barnaby went pink, which on a bear is very hard to see, but Pip saw it, and was kind.

“No harm done,” said Pip. “Some of us just get sleepy in the dark. Bears most of all, I think.”

So the next day he packed seven sandwiches instead of six. The seventh went on a plate beside a small pillow, with a card that read: For tunnel time.

The next time they went through the dark, there was one gentle, contented sound, and not a crumb anywhere else.

And at the top of the world, they say that if you listen carefully in the tunnels, you can still hear it. Pip says it is the happiest sound on the whole mountain.
