Hugo Delivers the Mail

A story from Coral Row

Scroll to turn the page.

Hugo was the only postman on Coral Row. Every morning he swam from shell to shell with a mailbag made from a sea sponge.

Mrs. Crab got bills, and pinched every one. The jellyfish twins got party invitations, and had to hold them tight so they would not float away.

The octopus got eight letters at a time, which was lucky, because she had eight arms. Everyone on Coral Row got mail.

Except, Hugo sometimes thought, for someone he could not quite name.

One Tuesday, a bottle bobbed into his bag with no name on it, no address, only a note on the cork: For whoever needs it.

Hugo carried it all morning. “Is this yours?” he asked. Mrs. Crab pinched it. The twins said theirs would be glittery. The octopus said it was not hers, and she had checked eight times.

At dusk he reached the quiet end of the reef, where Mabel the giant clam lived. Her shell had been shut for years, and she had never once received a letter.

Hugo felt a bit silly, leaving a bottle at the door of a clam. But he tapped anyway.

“Nobody has written to me in a very long time,” said a small voice from inside the shell. “I stopped checking.”

Hugo set the bottle on top of her shell, and waited, which is the other half of a postman’s job.

Slowly, the shell opened a crack, then a little more. Mabel reached out, took the bottle, and unrolled the note inside.

It said only: You are not forgotten. She read it three times. Then the pearl inside her shell lit up, as bright as a small moon.

The next morning, Hugo’s bag was so full he had to swim it over in three trips.

Mrs. Crab had written a very short letter, and not pinched it. The twins had written a glittery one. The octopus had written eight. The whole reef had gone and found a reason to say hello.

Now Mabel keeps her shell open all day, and her pearl glows over Coral Row like a streetlamp.

And once a month, Hugo writes one more note, with no name on it, and sets the bottle adrift. For whoever needs it. If you ever find one on the beach, it might be yours.
