The Dragon Who Couldn’t Roar

A very small, very loud story

Scroll to turn the page.

Everyone in Dragon Valley could roar. The big dragons roared at sunrise, to wake the hens, and at sunset, to tell the sun goodnight.

Pipkin, the smallest dragon, opened his mouth to roar too. And out came: squeak.

He tried gargling with pebbles. He tried shouting into a bucket. He even ate a whole red pepper, which made him sneeze.

The sneeze came out exactly the same. Squeak.

That evening, Pipkin sat alone on a ledge and watched the sun go down. Nobody noticed he was there, since he was so small.

“Maybe,” he said to himself, “I will just be a very good listener.”

Meanwhile, down at the stone bridge, a troll named Grumblegut had set up a very rude sign.

“Nobody crosses,” he rumbled, “unless they pay me a pie.” The villagers had no pies left, and the market lay on the other side.

The next morning, Pipkin crept to the edge of Echo Canyon and let out his tiny squeak.

And the canyon said it back. Squeak. Squeak. SQUEAK, forty times, from every direction, each one deeper than the last, until the whole valley rumbled.

Down at the bridge, Grumblegut froze. He heard forty enormous dragons, each one clearing its throat.

“Many... many dragons,” he whispered, and his knees began to knock.

Grumblegut dropped his club, dropped his sign, and ran so fast that he left a dust cloud shaped like a troll.

The bridge was open. The villagers cheered. The big dragons bowed. And nobody said a single word about Pipkin’s size.

“That,” said the biggest dragon, “is the best roar in the valley.”

“It’s a squeak,” said Pipkin. “Yes,” said the dragon. “But with echo.”

So every evening now, Pipkin goes to the edge of the canyon, squeaks once, and the whole valley says goodnight to the sun.
