Pocket and the Star Garden

A sequel from the rooftops

Scroll to turn the page.

The morning after the moon bloomed, something tinkled on the rooftop garden. It was a small silver seed, no bigger than a button, and it had landed in the biggest pot.

“A moon seed!” whispered Pocket. It glowed very faintly, like a thought.

It wanted to become a star, and a seed like that, as everyone knows, needs three things: water, a song, and someone to wait.

Water was easy. Pocket tipped his can, and the soil drank it. Waiting was harder.

He waited through sun, and through rain, and through a whole night of owls, and nothing happened at all.

“A song,” said Pocket. He only knew one, which was the clicking hum his wheels made. He hummed it anyway: tick-a-tick, tick-a-tock.

The soil gave a tiny wiggle. A green shoot poked out, and it glowed.

Then came a cold night with a hard, hard rain. Pocket held his umbrella over the shoot until his chest light dimmed to a flicker.

He did not move, not even when the wind tried to take the umbrella away.

By morning the shoot had grown into a tall stem with a bud at the very top.

At midnight it opened, one silver point at a time, until a small star was blooming on the roof.

Looking up, Pocket saw something he had never noticed. In the sky was a dark gap, where a star ought to be.

The little flower rose, light as a sigh, and slid into the gap with a soft, bright click.

Now, on clear nights, if you look above the chimneys, there is a star that blinks twice before the rest, as if to say hello.

That is Pocket’s star. He waves back with his watering can, every single time.
