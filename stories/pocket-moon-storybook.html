Pocket Waters the Moon

A story from the rooftops

Scroll to turn the page.

On top of the tallest building in a city of chimneys lived a small tin robot named Pocket, whose job was watering the rooftop garden.

Every evening he rolled between the pots with his copper can, and every evening he looked up at the moon, thin as a fingernail clipping.

Each night the moon grew thinner, until one night there was no moon at all.

“It’s not going away,” said Pocket. “It’s a flower, closing up. And it must be very thirsty.” The city told him that moons do not get thirsty. But, as Pocket pointed out, nobody had asked the moon.

So Pocket built a rocket. It took one copper kettle, two umbrellas, and the last firework from midsummer night.

He filled his watering can with all the rain the garden had saved since March, tied it to the side, and climbed in.

Up went the kettle, through the chimney smoke, past the sleeping pigeons, and into the dark where the stars were.

The stars blinked at him, one at a time, the way a crowd does when a stranger walks in. Pocket waved with his free hand.

The moon was a silver bud as big as a town, shut tight and cold as a spoon. Pocket rolled all the way around it and found nothing to knock on.

So he did the only thing he knew how to do. He tipped his can, and one drop fell on the silver, hissed, and glittered.

Then the bud began to open. First one petal, then another, each one unfolding with a sound like a long, slow yawn, until light was pouring out of the moon.

Pocket poured out every last drop. When the can was empty, he found that his chest light had gone dim too. He had used up all his power on the rocket.

But a robot, as it happens, drinks light. The moonbeam touched his chest, and the little lamp in him grew bright and warm.

And then the moonbeam did something nobody expected. It reached all the way down to the roof, and Pocket slid home along it, laughing the whole way.

So if you ever see a thin moon, don’t worry. Pocket is on his way up with his can.

And if you see a full one, give it a wave. He is probably sitting on the rim, drinking the light.
