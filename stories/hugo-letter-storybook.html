Hugo Gets a Letter

A story from Coral Row

Scroll to turn the page.

Hugo had delivered the mail on Coral Row for years, to everyone but himself. Nobody writes to the postman.

Hugo said he did not mind. (He minded, a little.)

Then one morning, a bottle bobbed up to his own mailbox. The cork was sealed. On the tag, in careful handwriting, were three words he had never seen before: To the Postman.

He unrolled it very slowly. “Dear Whoever, I found your bottle on the beach. I was having a very bad day, and your note made it a little better. Thank you. Who are you?”

It was signed Mira, and underneath she had added: P.S. I like your handwriting.

Hugo read it eleven times. But there was a problem. Mira lived up on the beach, where the sea ended, and a seahorse cannot swim on sand.

How do you answer a letter when you can’t get to the shore?

So he asked Coral Row. Mrs. Crab tied a knot that no wave could undo. The jellyfish twins sprinkled glitter, for luck. The octopus wrote eight postscripts, and Hugo gently took out seven.

“Now,” said Mrs. Crab, “we ask the afternoon tide.”

The tide, who carries anything for anyone who asks politely, picked up the bottle and rolled it along. Over the shallows, past the rocks, and up toward the shore, where the sunset was turning the sand to gold.

In the morning, a girl in a sun hat found a bottle at the edge of the water. Inside was a note with a small drawing of a seahorse in a postman’s cap.

Mira read it twice, and laughed, and held it up to the sun. “Hello, Hugo,” she said.

Now, once a week, the tide brings a letter to the little shell house at the end of Coral Row. The mailbox has a flag, and for the first time in years, it is always up.

Because the postman has a pen friend, and she writes back.
