Wilbur Is Afraid of the Dark

A not-very-scary story from Number 13, Hollow Lane

Scroll to turn the page.

Wilbur had haunted the attic of Number 13 for two hundred years, and he was very good at it. He could moan. He could rattle. He could drift through a wall without even saying sorry.

There was just one problem. When the lights went out, the dark was really dark, and Wilbur was afraid of it. Which is awkward, because ghosts only come out at night.

Every night he slept in an old trunk with the lid shut and one eye peeking out.

He had tried a night-light, but his hands went straight through it. He had tried counting sheep, but the sheep were also ghosts, and that was worse.

One night, a family moved into Number 13. They had a boy named Theo, whose bedroom was right underneath the attic. Theo was afraid of ghosts.

He heard a rattling above his ceiling, and pulled the blanket up to his nose. Up in the attic, the rattling was only Wilbur’s knees, knocking together.

Then a great storm rolled over Hollow Lane and every light on the street went out. Wilbur, trembling, floated downstairs to look for Theo’s night-light.

At the same moment, Theo crept into the hallway with his flashlight. They met at the bottom of the stairs. “AAAH!” said the ghost. “AAAH!” said the boy. The flashlight dropped, rolled away, and went out.

In the pitch dark, something strange happened. A soft, pale glow filled the hallway, like the moon behind a cloud.

“You’re… glowing,” said Theo. “I am?” said Wilbur. He looked down at himself, and for the first time in two hundred years, he opened both eyes. “I’m a night-light! I was the night-light all along!”

“You’re not very scary,” said Theo. “Thank you?” said Wilbur. “I’ve been practising for two hundred years.”

So they sat together on the stairs, the ghost lighting the way and the boy keeping him company, and they listened to the storm until it ran out of things to say.

When the morning came and the lights flickered back on, nobody noticed. Theo was asleep on the bottom step, and Wilbur was asleep beside him, snoring a little, in a very ghostly way.

Now, every night, Wilbur floats to Theo’s door and says “boo,” very gently. And Theo says, “boo yourself,” and goes to sleep with a ghost-shaped glow in the corner.

The house still creaks. The attic still rattles. But if you ever hear a knocking in the night, it is only Wilbur’s knees, and he would love it if you said goodnight.
