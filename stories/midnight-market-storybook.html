Marnie and the Midnight Market

A magical story for those who cannot sleep

Scroll to turn the page.

Marnie could not sleep. She had counted sheep, then the sheep’s lambs, then the lambs’ lambs, and the clock said it was exactly midnight.

Then something floated past her window: a small paper lantern, with a face.

“Follow me,” said the lantern, in a voice like rustling paper. “Mind the third step.”

Marnie put her coat over her pyjamas and followed it down the stairs, out the door, and along a lane she had never seen before, though she had lived there all her life.

At the end of the lane stood the Midnight Market, which only opens when the clock strikes twelve, and only for people who cannot sleep.

There were hundreds of lanterns, and stalls striped like peppermints, and the whole place smelled of rain and warm toast.

The first stall sold bottled thunder. “Please keep the lid on,” said the owl behind the counter, who wore a waistcoat and very small spectacles. “It sulks if it’s dropped.”

Inside the jars, tiny storms rumbled and flashed, politely.

The next stall sold moonbeams by the metre, to hang in windows. The one after that sold second-hand rainbows, a little faded, and half a spool of sunset.

Marnie touched everything with one finger, and was very, very careful.

At the very last stall sat the same owl (he got around), selling spare dreams. “What does a dream cost?” asked Marnie.

“Just one worry,” said the owl. “Something you’ve been carrying about.” Marnie thought, then handed over the worry that she would never, ever fall asleep.

“It’s a good one,” said the owl. “It’s got a whale in it.” Marnie yawned.

The market was already thinning out, stalls turning to mist, lanterns drifting away. “Home,” said her lantern. And this time, Marnie did not need to be told twice.

She was asleep before her head touched the pillow. Above her bed floated a small round dream, and inside it a whale swam slowly through a sky full of stars.

The lantern hung outside the window until morning, and then, as lanterns do, it was gone. But the market, Marnie knows, will be open again tonight.
