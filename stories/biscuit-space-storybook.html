Biscuit and the Lost Star

A space adventure

Scroll to turn the page.

Biscuit was a small corgi with a big imagination, and every night she lay in the garden and looked at the stars.

One night, one of them fell out of the sky and landed in her flowerbed. Fwoomp.

It was no bigger than an apple, and it was crying tiny sparkles. “I fell off the Great Dog,” it sniffled. “I’m the very tip of his tail, and I can’t get back.”

Biscuit looked up. And there he was, a dog made of stars, with a gap where his tail should end.

“Don’t worry,” said Biscuit, who had never been to space but was a very confident dog. “I’ll take you home.”

She built a rocket from her own doghouse, with fins from the garden gate and a goldfish bowl for a helmet. Across the door she painted: BISCUIT’S SPACE KENNEL. Please knock.

“Three, two, one… WOOF!” Up went the doghouse, past the rooftops, past the clouds, and out into the dark where the asteroids tumbled like giant potatoes.

Biscuit steered left, then right, then a bit left again, and only bumped one.

Halfway there, a grumpy comet blocked the way. “Nobody passes,” it growled. “I’ve had a very long day, and my tail is full of dust.”

The little star hid behind Biscuit’s ear. But Biscuit only tilted her head and thought.

Biscuit knew what to do about grumpy folks. She shared her biscuit.

The comet went quiet. It took a crunchy bite and said, “Oh. That is actually very good.” Then it swished its tail aside, and the little rocket zoomed through.

At last they reached the Great Dog, a huge, sleepy shape of light with a gap where his tail should end.

The little star hopped out, took a deep breath, and slotted into place with a soft ping. And the whole constellation gave a great, happy wag.

Now, when Biscuit lies in the garden at night, she wags her tail, and way up in the sky, the Great Dog wags his back.

On a clear night, look for the very tip of his tail. It twinkles a little more than the rest. That is the star, saying thank you.
